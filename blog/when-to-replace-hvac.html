7 Signs Your HVAC System Needs to Be Replaced (Not Just Repaired)

YT
By YETI Team · Dover, PA

We get this question all the time: "Should I repair this thing or just replace it?" And honestly, it's a fair question — a new HVAC system is a big investment, and nobody wants to spend money they don't have to. But at the same time, throwing good money after bad repairs on a dying system can cost you way more in the long run.

Here's the straight truth from the field. These are the seven signs we look for when customers ask us whether to repair or replace.

1. Your System Is More Than 15 Years Old

Most furnaces and air conditioning systems are designed to last 15–20 years with proper maintenance. Once you cross that threshold, reliability drops fast and efficiency tanks. If your system is 15+ years old and anything significant goes wrong, replacement almost always makes more financial sense than repair — especially with how much more efficient modern systems are.

A new high-efficiency system can reduce your heating and cooling costs by 20–40% compared to an older unit running at degraded efficiency.

Quick Rule of Thumb

Multiply the repair cost by the age of the system. If that number exceeds $5,000, it's almost always time to replace. This is called the Rule of 5,000 in the HVAC industry.

2. Your Energy Bills Keep Climbing

If your heating and cooling bills have been creeping up year after year — even though you're using your system the same way — that's a strong signal the system is losing efficiency. Aging components have to work harder to deliver the same output, which means they burn more fuel or electricity.

Pull out your utility bills from the last few winters and summers. If costs have gone up 15–20% or more without a corresponding change in usage or rates, your system is likely the culprit.

3. Frequent Repairs in the Past Two Years

One repair is normal. Two in the same season is a warning sign. If your HVAC has needed multiple service calls in the past 24 months — especially if those calls involved parts like the heat exchanger, compressor, or control board — you're likely in the death spiral of an aging system.

"We tell people: if you've spent more than half the cost of a new system on repairs in the last two years, the math almost never works out in favor of continuing to repair."

— YETI Heating & Cooling Team

4. The System Uses R-22 Refrigerant

R-22 (Freon) was phased out in the U.S. as of January 2020. If your AC still uses R-22, it's not only old — it's expensive to service. The remaining supply of R-22 has made it extremely costly, and any technician who tells you they can still keep your R-22 system running cheaply isn't being straight with you.

Modern systems use R-410A or newer refrigerants that are readily available and far more environmentally responsible. If your AC uses R-22, replacement is almost certainly the right call.

5. You're Getting Hot/Cold Spots Throughout the House

Uneven temperatures — some rooms stuffy, others freezing — can point to ductwork issues, but they can also mean your HVAC system is no longer sized appropriately for your home or has lost the capacity to distribute air properly. When a unit starts to fail, inconsistent temperatures are often one of the first signs.

6. Strange Noises or Smells

A well-maintained HVAC system should be pretty quiet. If you're hearing banging, grinding, squealing, or rattling, something is mechanically wrong. Similarly, burning smells, musty odors, or rotten egg smells are serious warning signs that need immediate attention.

Safety Note

A sulfur or rotten egg smell near your furnace could indicate a gas leak. Turn off your system, leave your home, and call your gas company immediately. Don't ignore it.

7. The System Runs Constantly but Can't Keep Up

If your furnace or AC is running almost non-stop and you're still uncomfortable, the system has either lost capacity or is so undersized for your home that it can never satisfy the thermostat. Either way, this causes your energy bills to skyrocket while your comfort suffers. It's a classic sign the system is on its last legs.
