Spring AC Prep: 6 Things to Do Before You Turn It On

Every year we get a wave of calls in June from people whose AC didn't work when they flipped it on for the first time. A lot of those calls could have been avoided with about an hour of prep work in April or May. Here's exactly what to check before the heat hits.

1. Change or Clean the Air Filter

This is the single most important thing you can do for your AC's health and efficiency. A dirty filter restricts airflow, makes the system work harder, and can cause it to freeze up or break down entirely. Replace or clean your filter before every cooling season — and honestly, check it monthly once summer is in full swing.

For most homes, a MERV 8–11 filter is the sweet spot. High enough to filter meaningful particulates, not so restrictive that it starves your system of air.

2. Clear Around the Outdoor Unit

Your condenser (the outdoor unit) needs at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides for proper airflow. After a winter of leaf buildup, mulch spreading, and plant growth, this often needs attention. Clear away any debris, trim back shrubs or overgrowth, and straighten any fins that may have gotten bent during winter. You can gently straighten bent fins with a butter knife.

Pro Tip

Rinse the condenser coils with a garden hose from the inside out (if you can access them safely) to remove dust and debris buildup. Never use a pressure washer — it'll damage the fins.

3. Check the Condensate Drain Line

Your AC pulls moisture out of the air and that water has to go somewhere — usually through a condensate drain line. Over winter, algae and mold can clog these lines. Find the drain line (usually a white PVC pipe near your indoor unit) and pour a cup of diluted bleach or white vinegar down it to flush it out. A clogged drain line leads to water damage or a shutdown of your system.

4. Test Your Thermostat

Before the heat of summer, switch your thermostat to "Cool" and set it 5 degrees below your current room temperature. Listen for the system to kick on. If nothing happens within a few minutes, check your circuit breaker. If the breaker is fine and it still won't start, call us before it gets hot.

Also: if you haven't replaced your thermostat batteries in the last 12 months, do it now. A dead thermostat battery is a surprisingly common cause of no-cooling calls.

5. Check Your Vents and Registers

Walk through your home and make sure all supply and return air vents are open and unobstructed. Furniture sitting on top of vents, closed dampers, or blocked returns force your system to work against itself. While you're at it, vacuum any visible dust off the vent covers.

6. Schedule a Professional Tune-Up

The five steps above are great owner maintenance — but nothing replaces a professional inspection. A tune-up includes checking refrigerant levels, testing electrical connections, lubricating moving parts, testing capacitors, and verifying that the system is operating within spec. Catching a worn capacitor or low refrigerant charge before the season starts costs a fraction of what a midsummer breakdown costs.
